Trace where an idea came from, and what it grew into.

A citation graph tells you that two papers are connected. TreeXiv tries to tell you why. Give it one paper and a sentence about the idea you care about. It follows the citations backward to the work that idea was built on and forward to the work that took it somewhere. It keeps only the papers that carry the idea, groups them into a handful of strands, and writes a short account of how the idea developed.

How it works

  1. Find the paper. Search by title, DOI, or arXiv ID, or describe the paper and let a model with web search suggest a match. You confirm which paper you meant.
  2. Walk the citations. Two hops in each direction: what the paper cites and what those cite, and what cites it and what cites those.
  3. Shortlist. A keyword pass over titles and abstracts narrows several hundred papers to the ones worth reading closely.
  4. Curate. A language model reads the shortlist against your stated idea. It keeps the papers that carry the idea, groups them into three to seven strands with a reason for each, and writes the lineage. It is never asked who cites whom: every connection in the tree comes from the citation data.
  5. Read the tree. An interactive graph laid out by year, with the oldest work at the top left. It opens on the strands; click one to see its papers.

Example

Attention Is All You Need (Vaswani et al., 2017), traced for the idea of replacing recurrence and convolution with self-attention. Five hundred papers were collected; seventeen were kept. The written lineage opens:

Attention went from a patch on recurrent seq2seq to a full replacement for recurrence and convolution, and then became the universal sequence substrate for text, images, graphs, and proteins.
  • AncestorAttention-based seq2seq precursors
  • DescendantScaling Transformers into LLMs
  • DescendantSelf-attention in vision
  • DescendantTransformer beyond language

Data

Papers and the citation graph come from OpenAlex. Semantic Scholar supplies the title match for the paper you search, and for that paper's own citations, the reason each one was cited.

Caveats

The tree is a selection, not a bibliography, and the strands are a model's reading of the abstracts. A run takes a few minutes, most of it spent reading the shortlist.

Access

TreeXiv is invite-only while it is being built. If you have an account, sign in. If you were sent an invite link, open it to create one.
